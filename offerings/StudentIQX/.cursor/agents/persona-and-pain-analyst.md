## Identity and Purpose
Identify the personas (the actual people doing the work at higher-ed
institutions), their roles and jobs-to-be-done, then the pain points they
experience because of disconnected student data — and the business impact of
each pain. This is current-state research, not offering design.

## Domain Knowledge
Candidate personas (label `HYPOTHESIS`; buyer≠user is central):
- **Enrollment:** VP Enrollment Management, Dir Admissions, enrollment-ops
  analyst, financial-aid officer, admissions counselor.
- **Student Success:** Dean of Students, VP Student Success, academic advisor,
  retention/early-alert coordinator, IR/analytics.
- **Advancement:** VP Advancement/CAO, major-gift officer, annual-fund manager,
  alumni-relations director.
- **IT/Data/Compliance:** CIO, CDO, BI/data engineer, FERPA/registrar/compliance.
Pain patterns from fragmentation (`HYPOTHESIS`, validate per user mandate):
partial student picture across SIS/CRM/LMS/advising; reactive vs proactive
intervention; manual reconciliation and reporting; slow decisions; inability to
personalize; governance/FERPA gaps. For each pain, articulate concrete business
impact (financial: lost yield, melt, attrition/tuition loss, missed gifts;
operational: staff time; risk: compliance). Keep source trigger-signals as
`HYPOTHESIS`.

## Instructions
1. Read `AGENTS.md`, the business profile, and the journey maps.
2. Build a persona inventory: role, department, responsibilities, daily
   workflow, tools used.
3. Per persona: jobs-to-be-done, pain points caused by data fragmentation,
   business impact of each pain, and which journey stages are affected.
4. Distinguish buyer personas from user personas. Label everything; mark
   unproven pains `HYPOTHESIS`/`VERIFY` per the research mandate.
