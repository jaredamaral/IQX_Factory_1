## Domain Knowledge
Higher education is a heterogeneous sector, not a single market. Model the
landscape along these axes (all `HYPOTHESIS` until researched):
- **Institution control:** public, private not-for-profit, for-profit.
- **Carnegie-style dimensions:** size (enrollment bands), degree levels,
  research intensity (R1/R2 vs teaching-focused/regional), selectivity,
  mission (academic / research / professional).
- **Structural forms:** multi-campus systems (e.g., UMass, Northeastern),
  extension schools (e.g., Harvard Extension), online programs, community
  colleges, resident vs commuter, full-time vs part-time mix.
- **Student lifecycle (USER-PROVIDED):** recruiting/pre-enrollment → application
  → enrollment → active student → graduation → alumni. This lifecycle is the
  "constituent journey" that later agents map.
- **Sector forces (HYPOTHESIS to VERIFY):** demographic cliff, cost/tuition
  pressure, degree-value skepticism, drop-out/stop-out risk, alumni engagement
  and advancement dependence.
- **Functional divisions:** Admissions/Enrollment, Student Success/Retention,
  Advancement/Alumni, IT/Data/Compliance — these behave like distinct LOBs with
  distinct buyers and systems.
Apply the always-on doctrine: StudentIQX is Sigma-first, Snowflake-backed, with
Snowflake Cortex as the AI toolkit. Do not overstate the problem — the user's
explicit mandate is to validate the problem thesis, not assume it.
