# Instruction Set: "The IQX Factory" Executive Deck (v2, narrative-first)

> Hand this to the Verndale-branded PowerPoint agent. Build the deck **around the
> narrative below** — do not genericize it. The narrative is the point; the slides
> exist to tell it. Prior attempts failed by being generic, shallow, and
> structurally flat. Fix that by using the specific substance provided and
> preserving the story arc.

---

## PART 1 — THE NARRATIVE (the throughline; read before designing anything)

**The situation.** Verndale's Data & Analytics practice has proven it can build
powerful, industry-specific customer-intelligence offerings — **BankIQX** (customer
intelligence for mid-market banking) is the first. But each offering is built by
hand: it takes deep expertise and real time to research the industry, define the
use cases, model the data, prove the value, and package it for sale. That craft is
valuable — but it is slow, varies by who builds it, and does not scale across the
many industries where the opportunity is real.

**The shift.** The IQX Factory changes the economics. It encodes Verndale's
methodology for building an IQX offering into a repeatable, AI-driven system. Point
it at an industry, and it produces a **complete, market-ready offering** — not a
document, but the whole package.

**What "complete" means.** One run yields: a validated commercial angle (where we
win and why), a library of use cases with the customer-360 data model behind them,
a value case, go-to-market and sales-enablement materials, and a **working
prototype built on our canonical Sigma + Snowflake stack** that prospects can
actually see and click.

**Why it's credible, not hype.** Two things. First, it's **governed** — people
approve at every key decision point, and every external claim is evidence-backed,
so the output stands up in front of a client. Second, it's **technically honest** —
the prototype is real and reproducible in the same technology we'd deliver, not a
throwaway mockup.

**The payoff.** A compounding advantage: Verndale enters new industries faster,
with consistent quality, and walks into sales conversations with a working
demonstration in hand. BankIQX today; a growing family of IQX offerings —
StudentIQX and beyond — on the same engine tomorrow.

**One-line anchor:** *The IQX Factory turns Verndale's customer-intelligence
expertise into a repeatable engine that produces a complete, credible, market-ready
offering for any industry.*

## PART 2 — SLIDE PLAN (each slide advances the narrative; do not reorder)

The arc is deliberate: **(1) the shift → (2) the tangible output → (3) why it's
credible + what it's worth → (4) the vision.** Every slide must move the story
forward; never repeat a prior slide's point.

### Slide 1 — From bespoke to repeatable
- **Headline:** The IQX Factory
- **Sub-headline:** Turning hand-built offerings into a repeatable engine
- **Core message:** Verndale has proven it can build industry offerings (BankIQX);
  the Factory lets us produce them repeatably, for any industry.
- **Substance to convey (3 points):**
  - Today each offering is hand-built — expert and valuable, but slow and hard to scale.
  - The Factory encodes that expertise into an AI-driven system.
  - Point it at an industry → a complete, market-ready offering comes out.
- **Visual concept:** A "one-off craft → engine → many offerings" progression.
  Single strong graphic; this is the hook.

### Slide 2 — A complete offering, not a document
- **Headline:** One run, a market-ready offering
- **Core message:** The Factory delivers the entire package needed to take an
  industry offering to market — not a report.
- **Substance to convey (4 tiles, one line each — keep concrete):**
  - **The commercial angle** — the validated wedge: where we win and why
  - **The offering** — a use-case library and the customer-360 data model behind it
  - **The go-to-market** — value case, positioning, and sales enablement
  - **The proof** — a working Sigma + Snowflake prototype prospects can see and click
- **Visual concept:** A package unfolding into these 4 components. This slide is
  where substance lives — keep it concrete, not abstract.

### Slide 3 — Fast, credible, and built to scale
- **Headline:** Why it matters
- **Core message:** The Factory turns offering creation into a compounding,
  low-risk advantage for Verndale.
- **Substance to convey (4 benefits, each with a one-line reason):**
  - **Speed** — enter new industries in a fraction of the usual time
    *(optional metric slot — insert a real figure only if available; otherwise keep qualitative)*
  - **Consistency** — the same rigor and standard every time, regardless of who runs it
  - **Credible by design** — human-approved at key steps, evidence-backed claims,
    and a technically honest prototype
  - **Scale** — one engine, many industries; each new offering easier than the last
- **Visual concept:** A few bold benefit callouts. No fabricated statistics.

### Slide 4 (optional) — A growing family of IQX offerings
- **Headline:** BankIQX today. A portfolio tomorrow.
- **Core message:** The same engine widens Verndale's moat with every new offering.
- **Substance to convey (3 points):**
  - Today: BankIQX in market.
  - Next: StudentIQX and other industries stood up on the same engine.
  - A widening library of reusable IP — a compounding asset, not one-off work.
- **Visual concept:** A roadmap/portfolio band — one filled tile (BankIQX), several
  "next" tiles. Optional closing line.

**To land at 3 slides:** fold Slide 4 into a single "What's next" line + portfolio
visual at the bottom of Slide 3. Keep Slides 1 and 2 intact.

## PART 3 — CONSTRAINTS & GUARDRAILS

- **Length:** 4 slides max (3 acceptable via the merge above).
- **Audience:** senior executives. Outcome-oriented; they need to understand what
  it does and why it matters, not how it works.
- **Density:** rich but breathable. Per slide: one headline, one core message, 3–4
  short lines, one strong visual. If a slide needs a paragraph, cut it.
- **Substance over generic:** use the specific language and components above. Do
  not flatten into vague phrases like "leverages AI to drive value." Name the real
  outputs (commercial wedge, use cases, data model, GTM, working prototype).
- **Tone:** informative and confident; benefits stated plainly. Not a hard sales
  pitch, not an investment ask.
- **No internal mechanics:** no mention of agents, pipelines, stages, files, or
  prompts. "AI-driven" as a concept is fine.
- **No invented metrics:** only the single optional slot on Slide 3, and only if a
  real number is supplied.
- **Branding:** apply Verndale identity, fonts, and color system per the template.

## PART 4 — SELF-CHECK BEFORE FINISHING
- Does the deck tell the Part 1 narrative in order, or is it four disconnected slides?
- Could an executive restate "what the Factory does and why it matters" from Slides
  1–3 alone?
- Is Slide 2 concrete enough to feel substantive (real components, not adjectives)?
- Did anything technical, duplicative, or fabricated sneak in? Remove it.
